I. Title: Extended Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing Index Modulation with Precoding Matrix and Low-Complexity Detection Designs
II. Abstract: This paper introduces a novel extended orthogonal frequency division multiplexing index modulation (S-OFDM-IM) scheme that employs precoding matrices such as the Walsh-Hadamard (WH) and Zhao-Zhu (ZC), to extend non-zero data symbols and their indexes on active subcarriers, and then compresses them into all available subcarriers. The aim is to enhance transmission diversity performance by simultaneously leveraging multipath diversity and index diversity.
In terms of performance analysis, this paper derives the bit error probability (BEP) to gain a deeper understanding of diversity gains and coding gains, particularly analyzing the impact of different extended matrices on these gains. It has been found that using the rotated versions of the original WH and ZC matrices can further improve BEP performance — the rotation matrix enables S-OFDM-IM to achieve maximum diversity gain (i.e., the number of subcarriers), while the baseline scheme's diversity gain is typically limited to 2.
Furthermore, this paper proposes three low-complexity detectors: the minimum mean square error log likelihood ratio detector (MMSE-LLR), the index pattern minimum mean square error detector (IP-MMSE), and the enhanced index pattern minimum mean square error detector (EIP-MMSE), which can achieve varying degrees of complexity and reliability balance. Simulation results show that the performance of S-OFDM-IM outperforms various benchmark schemes.
III. Introduction: Index modulation (IM) is an emerging technology that uses the index of activated channels to convey information in addition to traditional M-level modulation symbols. The concept of IM was initially proposed in code division multiple access systems and later applied to orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) technology, leading to the development of orthogonal frequency division multiplexing index modulation (OFDM-IM) schemes.
OFDM-IM only activates a subset of subcarriers, carrying data bits through M-ary complex data symbols and activated subcarrier indices. By adjusting the number of activated subcarriers, it achieves a balance between spectral efficiency (SE) and reliability, offering higher energy efficiency (EE) and reliability compared to traditional OFDM. This has attracted widespread attention from researchers in recent years.
In existing research, scholars have optimized the performance of OFDM-IM through various means, such as relaxing the number of activated subcarriers to increase index bits, designing greedy detectors, analyzing bit error rate (BER) under channel state information (CSI) uncertainty, exploring applications in multi-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems, etc. Meanwhile, there are also proposals to enhance the transmission diversity of OFDM-IM, such as coordinated interleaving OFDM-IM (CI-OFDM-IM), compression-sensing-assisted OFDM-IM, etc. However, these solutions may be complex or have limited diversity gain.
Although precoding / extension techniques have matured in traditional OFDM for effectively enhancing multipath diversity gain, there has not been sufficient exploration in literature to fully exploit the potential of precoding OFDM-IM to simultaneously utilize both multipath diversity and index diversity. Based on this, this paper proposes an S-OFDM-IM scheme to maximize the transmission diversity performance of OFDM-IM.
Conclusion: This paper introduces a novel S-OFDM-IM scheme that incorporates various expansion matrices into the OFDM-IM framework to enhance its reliability. Specifically, the scheme uses WH and ZC expansion matrices to expand non-zero data and index symbols, and then compresses them into all subcarriers, thereby simultaneously utilizing both multipath diversity and index diversity, significantly improving the transmission diversity performance of OFDM-IM.
By analyzing the Bit Error Probability (BEP), this paper clarifies the impact of different expansion matrices on diversity gain and coding gain, and finds that using the rotation versions of WH and ZC matrices can further enhance performance — these rotation matrices enable S-OFDM-IM to achieve maximum diversity gain, with the performance of the ZC matrix rotation consistently optimal.
Furthermore, three low complexity detectors proposed in this paper (MMSE-LLR, IP-MMSE, EIP-MMSE) have a complexity independent of the modulation order M. Simulation results show that even with the use of low complexity detectors, the performance of S-OFDM-IM still surpasses various benchmark schemes.
